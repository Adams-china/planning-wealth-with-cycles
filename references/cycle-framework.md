# Cycle framework

## Cycle hierarchy

| Horizon | Approximate lens | Decision use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40–60 years | technology, institutions, global price regimes | strategic themes only |
| 15–25 years | housing, demographics, construction | property and credit context |
| 7–11 years | capital expenditure and credit | risk-budget adjustment |
| 3–4 years | inventory, orders, production | entry pacing and rebalancing |

Lengths are ranges, not clocks. Conflicting signals are normal.

## Observable regime grid

Classify direction, not merely level, using growth, inflation, liquidity/credit, valuation, and supply constraints.

| Regime | Typical tendency | Important exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growth up, inflation down | quality equities and duration benefit | expensive valuations can overwhelm the regime |
| Growth up, inflation up | cyclicals and commodities may lead | tightening can end the move quickly |
| Growth down, inflation up | cash, gold, defensive quality gain relevance | bonds can suffer if inflation expectations rise |
| Growth down, inflation down | high-quality bonds and liquidity help | credit risk and policy response determine outcomes |

## Asset tendencies

- **A-shares:** combine domestic credit, policy, earnings revisions, valuation, and market structure.
- **US equities:** combine earnings quality, innovation, valuation, concentration, rates, and currency exposure.
- **Gold:** treat as monetary/tail-risk insurance affected by real rates, currency credibility, central-bank demand, and geopolitics; it has no cash flow.
- **Bonds:** distinguish government, investment-grade, high-yield, duration, credit, and currency risks.
- **Cash:** value optionality and liability matching, not only nominal yield.
- **Property:** separate consumption from investment; test population flow, inventory, rent yield, financing, taxes, policy, and liquidity.
- **Commodities:** combine inventories, supply capex, demand impulse, dollar/real rates, geopolitics, and futures structure.

## Kondratiev interpretation

Preserve: technology diffusion and catch-up industrialization reshape long-run prices; supply investment lags can produce nonlinear commodity shocks; cycle horizons should serve different decisions; liquidity matters when expected returns fall; asset ownership can create larger wealth discontinuities than wage growth.

Reject as rules: exact 60-year periodicity; predetermined bottom/top years; a universal housing clock; overriding valuation, balance sheets, or household constraints with one cycle phase.

## Common errors

- Narrative fitting after prices move.
- Changing cycle boundaries to preserve a forecast.
- Confusing correlation with causation.
- Ignoring policy, war, pandemics, and institutional change.
- Using macro views to justify concentration or leverage.
